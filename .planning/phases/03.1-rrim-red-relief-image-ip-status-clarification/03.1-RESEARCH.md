## Summary

The blocking question is "can `rrim.py` be pushed to the public `gseg-ethz/pc2img` remote without infringing third-party IP?" The reported holder is confirmed: **Asia Air Survey Co., Ltd. (AAS)**, inventor **Tatsuro Chiba**, first filed **2002-11-05**. The decisive finding is about *timing*: the **core RRIM patent family — the "Visualizing system, method, and program" invention that actually claims the slope-red + openness-brightness composition — has EXPIRED in every jurisdiction** as of mid-2026. Japan `3670274`, China `CN1711568B`, Taiwan `I285852` are all listed expired on AAS's own patent page, and the US member **US 7,764,282 B2** shows *"Expired - Lifetime, adjusted expiration 2025-10-05"* on Google Patents — i.e. it lapsed at term end roughly nine months before today (2026-07-09). An expired patent's claimed invention is in the public domain; anyone may practice it without a licence. This substantially removes the patent barrier that motivated the gate and means the **preferred lean D-03 (keep) is now supportable on patent-expiry grounds without needing to obtain any research/educational licence from AAS** — which is a stronger and simpler basis than the CONTEXT's original D-03 premise assumed.

Two residual live-IP items remain and must be addressed in the findings doc rather than ignored: (1) AAS still holds **active patents on *different* inventions** — JP `5281518` (stereoscopic image generation, JP-only, to 2029) and US `11,836,856 B2` (super-resolution *stereoscopic* visualization, US-only, to 2040). Neither reads on `rrim.py` on a good-faith read, because `rrim.py` produces a single flat 2-D RGB raster (no stereo pair, no anaglyph, no super-resolution / smoothing-elevation step), but the good-faith doc should walk the actual independent claims. (2) **"RRIM" / "Red Relief Image Map" appears to be used as a trademark ("RRIM®")** by AAS — a *naming/branding* concern that is legally distinct from patents and is not cured by patent expiry; mitigate with an attribution NOTICE and (optionally) descriptive naming. Reinforcing the non-infringement position: `rrim.py`'s two ingredients are a universal *slope* primitive and *positive/negative topographic openness*, and openness is an **independent academic method (Yokoyama, Shirasawa & Pike, 2002)** with open-source implementations (SAGA-GIS), not AAS property.

Mechanically, the disposition is cheap to execute now: **`rrim.py` has never been pushed to `origin`** (verified — no remote-tracking ref contains its introducing commit `019e9ed`), it exists on only two local, linear, unpushed branches (`phase-03` ⊂ `phase-03.1`), and `develop-gsd`/`main` never carried it. So a D-05 strip needs no force-push coordination, and a D-03 keep needs no un-wiring (the module is already not imported by any `__init__.py`).

**Primary recommendation:** Plan for **D-03 (keep + attribution NOTICE + opt-in extra for optional deps)**, with the ADR grounding redistribution in **core-patent expiry** (not a licence grant), plus a trademark-attribution note and a documented good-faith non-infringement read against the two still-active AAS patents. Keep D-05 (strip-from-history) fully specced as the executable fallback in case the D-07 external reviewer (ETH tech-transfer) disputes the expiry read. Because the license-wait may now be unnecessary, surface to the owner that the D-07 "external wait" could collapse to an in-house documented sign-off — but that is an owner/discuss decision, not one to make unilaterally.

### Finding 2 — Infringement read (good-faith, NON-BINDING; not legal advice)

**What `rrim.py` actually computes** (verified by reading the source):
1. `compute_slope` — gradient magnitude of the raster (a universal, unpatentable primitive).
2. `compute_openness` — for `num_directions` rays out to `max_distance`, the mean over directions of `90° − max elevation angle` (**positive openness**) and `90° − max depression angle` (**negative openness**). This is exactly **Yokoyama, Shirasawa & Pike (2002)** topographic openness. `[CITED: asprs.org 2002 PE&RS 68(3):257-265]`
3. `structure = 0.5·(positive − negative)` — the ridge-valley / elevation-depression measure.
4. `compose_rrim_rgb` — normalize structure→brightness (base gray), slope→boost the **red** channel; output a single **flat 2-D RGB** raster.

**What the core (now-expired) claim requires** (US 7,764,282 / JP 3670274, per the claim summary): map coordinates → determine an **elevation degree** (≈ positive openness) → determine a **depression degree** (≈ negative openness) → **weighted synthesis** into an elevation-depression degree (≈ `structure`) → 2-D **tone indication** commensurate with it; a dependent "gradient reddening" embodiment adds slope→red channel from DEM + aboveground/underground opening. `[CITED: patents.google.com/patent/US7764282B2/en]`

**Good-faith read:** `rrim.py` **plausibly reads on the core claim's limitations** — it is a faithful image-space implementation of the same slope-red + (positive−negative)-openness-brightness composition. **But that patent family is expired**, so reading on it carries no infringement consequence. Against the two **still-active** AAS patents, `rrim.py` **does not appear to read**, because both add limitations `rrim.py` lacks (stereoscopic-pair/anaglyph generation for JP 5281518; super-resolution + smoothing-elevation for US 11,836,856) and both are single-jurisdiction (JP / US) whereas the maintainer is ETH Zurich (CH/EU). Openness being independent prior art (Yokoyama 2002, freely implemented in SAGA-GIS and public GitHub repos) further weakens any claim that computing openness alone is proprietary. `[ASSUMED — good-faith engineering read of claim summaries, explicitly not a legal opinion]`

### Finding 3 — Redistribution rights (the D-03 vs D-04 pivot — reframed)

The CONTEXT framed the pivot as "does an obtainable research/educational licence permit public redistribution?" **The patent-expiry finding changes the question.** For the *base RRIM technique* there is **no live patent to license** in JP/CN/TW/US — the redistribution basis is *"the claimed invention has entered the public domain on term expiry"*, which permits redistribution to all users (including commercial) inside a permissively-licensed public PyPI package **without any grant from AAS**. This is a *stronger* basis than a research/educational licence (which typically restricts commercial use) and it dissolves the D-03/D-04 split for patent reasons: there is no licence whose scope needs to be tested. `[VERIFIED: patents.google.com legal-status "Expired"]` + `[ASSUMED: public-domain-on-expiry is a standard patent-law principle, non-binding]`

**Residual redistribution caveats (must be handled, not license-shaped):**
- **Trademark, not patent.** AAS uses **"RRIM®"**; "Red Relief Image Map" functions as a brand. Trademark is unaffected by patent expiry. Nominative/descriptive use ("an implementation of the red-relief-image technique of Chiba et al.") is generally defensible, but the package should **not present itself as "RRIM®"** or imply endorsement. Mitigation: attribution NOTICE + optionally a descriptive internal name. This is the one item that could, in principle, motivate a *rename* (not a strip). `[ASSUMED — trademark analysis, non-binding]`
- **Two active patents** (Finding 1) — confirm the good-faith non-infringement read in the findings doc; territorial (JP/US) scope favors an ETH-based publisher.
- **D-07 external confirmation** remains prudent: **ETH tech-transfer** (ETH transfer / "Innovation & Entrepreneurship Lab") is the right body to sign off the expiry read before public push. Whether their sign-off is a *blocking wait* (as D-07 assumes) or an in-house-documented step is an **owner decision** (see Open Questions).

## Mechanical Deep-Dive: optional-dependency / plugin patterns (for D-03/D-04)

- **Import-time registration is the real gate, not extras.** `rrim.py` registers `RRIMFeature`/`RRIMPackFeature`/`RRIMComponentFeature` via `@FEATURES.register`, which fires **only when the module is imported**. It is currently **not** imported by `src/pc2img/features/__init__.py`, so RRIM is *already* not in the public feature barrel — it is loaded only by its test's `from pc2img.features import rrim`. `[VERIFIED: reading features/__init__.py wiring + test file]`
- **A pip `extra` does not withhold source.** Per D-06: an `[project.optional-dependencies] rrim = [...]` entry gates *optional dependencies*, not the `rrim.py` file, which ships in the wheel regardless. So "opt-in extra" is meaningful only for optional runtime deps and as a signposting convention — the legal basis (NOTICE + expiry) is what makes distribution safe. Use the existing `cuda11/cuda12` extras + PEP 735 `dev/doc` groups as the structural analog.
- **D-04 re-registration:** a separate `pc2img-rrim` package re-registers by importing its module; because registration is a global side-effect on `pc2img.features.registry.FEATURES`, `import pc2img_rrim` at the consumer's call site is sufficient — no entry-point plugin machinery is strictly required, though a `pc2img.features` entry point could auto-load it if desired (heavier; not needed for this scope).

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Citing the wrong US patent number
**What goes wrong:** US **7,264,282** is a thread-knotting patent; the RRIM patent is US **7,764,282**. A one-digit slip (which appears even on secondary summaries of AAS's page) puts a fabricated basis in the findings doc.
**How to avoid:** cite Google Patents `US7764282B2` directly; cross-check assignee = "Asia Air Survey Co Ltd" and inventor = "Tatsuro Chiba".
**Warning sign:** a "visualizing system" patent whose abstract talks about thread/knots.

### Pitfall 2: Treating "expired on the page" as still-active
**What goes wrong:** AAS's page (and Google Patents) label US 7,764,282 with an *expiration date* of 2025-10-05. Read literally as "expires in the future" it looks active — but **today is 2026-07-09**, so it has already lapsed. Google Patents' own status string is "Expired - Lifetime".
**How to avoid:** compare every expiry date against today's date; don't copy a page's tense.

### Pitfall 3: Assuming a pip extra prevents distribution (D-06)
**What goes wrong:** planning "ship RRIM behind an extra so users don't get it" — but the `.py` is in the wheel regardless; the extra only gates optional deps. A gate without a legal basis does not cure a distribution risk.
**How to avoid:** treat the NOTICE + expiry basis as the actual clearance; use the extra only for optional runtime deps.

### Pitfall 4: A working-tree delete instead of a history rewrite (D-05)
**What goes wrong:** `git rm rrim.py && commit` leaves the blob reachable in history; the todo explicitly forbids this because a public push would still carry it.
**How to avoid:** rewrite `019e9ed` + drop the two test commits; verify `git log --all -- <paths>` is empty and `git gc --prune=now`.

### Pitfall 5: Conflating patent expiry with trademark clearance
**What goes wrong:** concluding "patent expired ⇒ everything about RRIM is free," ignoring that "RRIM®" is a trademark. Patent expiry does not release a brand name.
**How to avoid:** add the trademark-attribution note; use descriptive framing; don't present the package as "RRIM®".
